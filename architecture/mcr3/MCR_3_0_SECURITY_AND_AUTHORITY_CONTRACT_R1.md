# MCR 3.0 Security and Authority Contract R1

## 1. Seven findings that are never interchangeable
1 hash correctness; 2 signature validity; 3 producer identity; 4 provider trust; 5 evidence freshness; 6 delegated execution authority; 7 outcome validation. `mcr3_provenance.assess()` decides 1 to 5 and records them in the event's `provenance`; `evc1.derive_trust()` turns that record into a trust level (a pure function, checked exhaustively over all 216 combinations). It decides **nothing** about 6 or 7. A signed, trusted, fresh event still has `authority_effect = NONE`.

Trust levels: UNTRUSTED_RESEARCH (no signature), SIGNATURE_INVALID, SIGNED_UNTRUSTED, SIGNED_TRUSTED, REVOKED, EXPIRED, REPLAYED. SIGNED_TRUSTED needs a valid signature over the event content digest, a hash-consistent event, a TRUSTED non-revoked issuer, a fresh attestation and an unseen (signer, content digest, signing time). Revocation wins over everything except replay. The signature binds the content digest, the signing time, the signer and, when the signer chooses, the policy and authority digests, so changing any of them invalidates it. Verifiers may pin the expected policy and authority digests.

Unsigned evidence is usable for research and observation, explicitly UNTRUSTED_RESEARCH; self-consistent evidence is never described as authenticated. Back-ends: HMAC (TEST ONLY; a shared secret proves no identity to a third party), Ed25519 (needs the optional `cryptography` package; recorded NOT_VERIFIED if absent), SSHSIG via `ssh-keygen -Y verify` (the mechanism the Claude Mods release verifier uses; NOT_VERIFIED if the tool is absent). **No production signing key was created or deployed.** Tests use throwaway identities in temporary folders.

## 2. Capability and adapter governance
States: DISCOVERED, QUARANTINED, VERIFIED, QUALIFIED, APPROVED, SUSPENDED, REVOKED. Unknown capabilities are quarantined on discovery. Verification needs a passing result and an evidence digest; qualification needs an independent GO or GO_WITH_DOCUMENTED_GAPS verdict, a named reviewer and an evidence digest. **Qualification never approves**: the registry has no method that reaches APPROVED except `record_approval`, which needs an approval record issued by an EXTERNAL approver (in the intended deployment, the Claude Mods Governor), verified by a pluggable `ApprovalVerifier` (default: deny everything), complete, unexpired and scoped to owner, capability, provider, target, operation, environment and policy digest. An expired approval silently lapses to QUALIFIED. Suspension clears the approval and requires re-verification; revocation is final; raising the policy epoch withdraws every approval. MCR does not create a competing approval system, and `view()` always reports `mcr_grants_authority: false`.

Adapters declare ADAPTER_ID, SOURCE_RUNTIME_VERSION, TARGET_CONTRACT_VERSION, SUPPORTED_CAPABILITIES, EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION, TRUST_REQUIREMENTS, QUALIFICATION_EVIDENCE, APPROVAL_STATUS, REVOCATION_STATUS and DEFAULT_AUTHORITY=NONE. A new adapter, and every new version, starts OBSERVATION_ONLY. Promotion needs qualification evidence and an external approval reference; revoked adapters cannot be promoted; a rollback never lifts a restriction that a revocation recorded; an unknown adapter gets the lowest mode.

## 3. Replay ledger: guarantees by platform
What is guaranteed (tested on Windows, local NTFS, threads and separate processes): every send is recorded and fsynced in the ledger BEFORE the call is made; a second send for the same (authority, mutation id) is refused; the per-authority send count is bounded; compensation attempts are counted against a separate grant, one ledger entry per attempt; a malformed, torn or corrupted ledger fails closed; ledger paths that name devices, alternate data streams, parent directories or drive-relative forms are refused on every platform.
**Anchor.** `ReplayLedger.anchor()` returns `{ledger_id, count, head}` where `head` is a hash chain over the entries; `check_anchor()` detects a restored older copy (fewer entries) and rewritten history (different head). The anchor only helps if it is stored where the ledger's host cannot rewrite it; leases carry it, and `resume()` checks it. Without an independently held anchor a restore or deletion is NOT detected.
Not guaranteed: POSIX locking (untested here, NOT_VERIFIED); network filesystems; callers using different ledger files for one authority; volatile ledgers across processes; power-loss durability (untested). **Local ledger tests do not establish exactly-once enterprise execution**, and the model does not limit how often a caller actually invokes a provider: a ledger entry means "MCR recorded a send", not "the provider executed once".

## 4. Compensation
A separate bounded grant (`max_mutations` attempts, never inheriting forward authority). Each attempt consumes its own ledger entry before the call. One attempt may be open at a time. A definite failure (not sent, provider error) allows the next attempt; an unknown outcome (no response, commit unknown) blocks the next attempt until an authoritative readback shows the goal still unmet. The last failed attempt makes the mission UNRECOVERABLE. Success needs a successful execution receipt bound to the grant and the attempt number, and a distinct authoritative post-compensation readback that satisfies the goal. Compensation evidence is persisted only after every guard passes. Residual: a post readback equal to the forward state plus a new field that the goal tests is accepted (no provenance).

## 5. Enforced fields and exact bindings (3.0 contract changes)
`expires_at` must be a valid UTC time (date, RFC 3339 `Z`, or a number) and is checked at authorization and again at the point of sending (instant = expired); `intent.expected_outcome_hash` must equal the frozen outcome contract's hash; readback contract `checks` must be a non-empty list of unique strings and every named check must be present and known in an EXISTS readback, else the commit stays unknown; predicates may test `$activation`; R51 requires `provider_ref == "<provider_id>::<tool>"` exactly (non-empty parts, no further `::`). Self-reported `roles` and `tenant` are not treated as verified identity: principal assurance is SELF_REPORTED unless SIGNED_TRUSTED evidence supports VERIFIED.

## 6. Credential-shape scanner
Detects Anthropic, OpenAI, GitHub (classic and fine-grained), GitLab, Google (API key, OAuth access token, service-account key), SendGrid, Stripe (key and webhook secret), AWS (key id and secret key assignment), Azure (storage key, SAS signature), Slack, Twilio, npm, PyPI, Hugging Face, JWT, PEM/PGP private-key blocks, Bearer/Basic headers, URL user-info, and secret-named assignments, in UTF-8, UTF-16/32 at every byte offset, and Latin-1. Findings carry the shape name and a short fingerprint, never the matched text. Synthetic fixtures are assembled at run time from fragments.
**Intentionally not detected (documented limits):** obfuscated or transformed secrets (zero-width characters, homoglyphs, base64 or URL-encoded wrappers, values split across lines or strings); a value shorter than 16 characters under a `token`/`authorization` name; a quoted password containing spaces; any secret matching none of the shapes. It is a heuristic and is not proof that the tree holds no secret.

## 7. Threat checklist (OWASP agentic-AI themes, checked as tests, not as a dependency)
Goal/authority hijack via evidence (authority_effect NONE; qualification never approves); tool misuse (exact provider binding, bounded attempts); identity and privilege abuse (roles not verified, approvals scoped and expiring); memory/evidence poisoning (hash chain, canonical digests, supersession without deletion, signature binding); cascading failure (UNKNOWN states fail closed); repudiation (append-only lineage, original and normalized provenance retained); supply chain (no new runtime dependency; optional libraries recorded NOT_VERIFIED when absent). Not covered: denial of service, side channels, hostile code inside the process (the library trust boundary is unchanged from R2.0).
